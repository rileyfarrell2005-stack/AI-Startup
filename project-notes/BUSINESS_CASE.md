## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** One specific customer for this app?
- **Problem:** what is their current problem?
- **Solution:** how they solve the current problem now , what we do better?
- **Revenue:** who pays the platform owner?

**Customer:** Homeowners and people who own appliances that need repairs.

**Problem:** Finding someone to repair an appliance can be difficult sometimes, especially when local technicians are busy or unavailable for several weeks at a time. Customers often have to seek their options out online, ask friends for recommendations or contact several repair businesses before finding someone who can help in a timely manner. This can take time, which people do not have if an essential appliance is in need of repair, as well as it can be difficult to compare prices and availability if everything is not centeralized.

**Solution:** FixerUpper is a platform that connects customers with independent appliance repair technicians. Customers can search for technicians based on their location, the type of appliance they can repair and their availability on specific dates. They can then submit a repair request, receive quotes and decide which technician best suits their needs at the given time. This is simpler than having to contact several businesses individually and allows customers to find and compare options in one place.

**Revenue:** The business would make money through advertising and revenue-sharing arrangements with technicians who use the platform to promote their services. There is a fee for customers as well for the convenience of this server. This gives independent technicians another way to reach potential customers while generating income for FixerUpper, and for customers generate income for FixerUpper by paying commissions with each job that is paid for. 

- $5 customer convenience fee per successful booking.
- $5 technician platform fee per successful booking.
- $10 total revenue per booking when both fees are collected.
  

## 2. Where IT creates value

**Which parts of the value chain does your product change, and how? Which course concepts apply (e.g. scale, distribution channels, differentiation, data, switching costs)?**

This product creates **value** by improving the scale of appliance repair businesses. Instead of relying mainly on word of mouth / local advertising / reputation, start-up technicians can use the platform to reach a larger customer base and generate more opportunities. This allows start-up technicians to compete with larger companies without needing to spend as much money on advertising.

**Distribution channels** are where the application provides technicians with another way to advertise their services and allows customers to find available repair professionals on one centralized platform. This makes the process increasingly convenient for both the technician, and the customer.

The platform also creates **value** through data. By tracking previous repair requests and fulfilled orders, appliance information and customer history, the application could make it easier for returning customers to access their repair records and find technicians who have experience with specific appliances based on past performance with new and returning customers. There are also ways to report issues when they come up.

Finally, the application could create **switching costs**. A customer may normally rely on a local technician or someone they know, but that person may not be available to come for several weeks. By having a platform that connects customers with multiple technicians, competitive pricing and easier booking. Over time, saved repair histories and previous bookings could make customers more likely to continue using the application rather than returning to their previous methods of finding repair services. This has a network effect by growing its value to each customer as more people use this app.


## 3. Network effects

Does your product get more valuable as more people use it?

- Is it a **one-sided** or **two-sided** market? Who are the sides?
- Where does the extra value come from as the network grows?
- How would you solve the "empty network" problem at launch?
- Point to the part of your prototype that shows this.

This business platform operates as a **two-sided market** because it connects two different groups into the same plaform: Appliance repair technicians and customers looking for appliance repairs. The purpose of the application is to bring these two groups together in one centralized location where they can interact and arrange repair services.

The platform exhibits **network effects** and becomes more valuable as more people begin using it. As more technicians join, customers have greater choice, availability and access to technicians who specialize in different appliance types. More customers, in turn, create more potential jobs for technicians, making the platform more attractive to other repair businesses. Customers can also compare availability, services and prices, creating competition between technicians that could lead to better pricing and faster service. This creates a positive feedback loop because growth on either side makes the platform more valuable to the other.

The main challenge when launching the business would be solving the empty network problem. Customers are unlikely to continue using an application if they open it and find very few available technicians. I would address this by focusing on getting qualified technicians first. Before publicly launching the application to customers, I would approach independent appliance repair technicians and offer the platform as a new and easy service to help them advertise their businesses and find additional customers. Once enough technicians and service listings were available, I would begin marketing the application to customers.

My prototype demonstrates this concept through its technician listings and service advertisements. These listings represent the supply side of this market, while the customers searching for specific appliance repairs represent the demand side. The goal is to build a network where groups benefit as participation increases.


## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

**Anyone in this class could rebuild your website in a weekend with the same AI tool. So the code is **not** your competitive advantage.**
**
- What about your business is **valuable, rare, hard to imitate and well-organized** (VRIN)?
- What would a competitor with a bigger budget need to copy you, and how long would it take?
- Which of your advantages get *stronger* over time (data, network, brand, switching costs)?


Although a competitor can copy the application itself, the real competitive advantage would come from the network of technicians and customers using the platform. The website and its features are relatively easy to **replicate**, but establishing **disruptive technology** for a repair service network and gaining customer trust would take more time.

Using the **resource based view**, the business could develop several competitive advantages. First, it provides **value** by helping customers find available technicians, compare prices and arrange repairs more efficiently. It also provides value to independent technicians by giving them another way to advertise their services and generate business. Second, the platform could become **rare** if it establishes a strong network of local technicians with verified qualifications, competitive pricing and reliable availability. Third, it would become more difficult to **imitate** as the business develops relationships with technicians, collects customer reviews and builds a history of completed repairs. Finally, the platform would need to be properly **organized** to take advantage of these resources through reliable listings, customer support, technician verification and an effective booking system.

A competitor with a larger budget could potentially develop a similar application and spend more money on advertising to attract customers. However, they would still need to recruit independent technicians, establish relationships with them, build customer trust and collect enough reviews to become a reliable alternative. Depending on the market, this could take months or even years to develop into an established network. Having a larger advertising budget does not automatically mean customers will trust or prefer their platform.

The competitive advantages that would become stronger over time are **network effects, data and brand recognition**. As more technicians and customers join, the platform becomes increasingly useful to both groups. The application would also accumulate valuable data about previous repairs, customer preferences, technician performance and service demand. This information could help improve the customer experience and make the platform more effective. Finally, positive customer experiences and reviews would help establish brand recognition and trust. Over time, these advantages could create higher **switching costs**, as customers become comfortable with the platform and technicians rely on it to generate business.

Ultimately, the competitive advantage is not the technology itself, but the established network, customer trust and information that develop around the business.


## 5. Cloud and operations

What would it take to run this for real? Which parts would you buy as cloud services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), and what are the risks of relying on them?

If I were running FixerUpper as a real business, I would need customer accounts, secure login, a shared database, payment processing and a way to keep track of repair requests and bookings. I would use a **PaaS (Platform as a Service)** provider to host the application and manage its database. This would allow customer accounts, technician listings and booking information to be stored in one place and accessed by both sides of the platform. It would also make it easier to handle more users as the business grows without having to purchase and maintain our own servers.

I would also use **SaaS (Software as a Service)** for services such as payment processing, email notifications and customer support. For example, a payment service could handle the customer's $5 convenience fee and the technician's $5 platform fee for each successful booking. Using existing services would save time and allow the business to focus more on connecting customers with repair technicians rather than building every system from scratch. I would not need to invest in my own physical servers at the beginning.

However, relying on third-party providers also creates risks. If a cloud provider experiences an outage, customers might temporarily lose access to technician listings or booking services. Providers could also increase their prices, making the business more expensive to operate. Another concern is vendor lock-in, which could make it difficult or costly to move the application and its data to another provider. To reduce these risks, I would maintain regular data backups, choose providers that allow data to be transferred and have alternative services available where possible. This would help keep FixerUpper dependable for both customers and technicians.
