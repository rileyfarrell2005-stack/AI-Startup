## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Anyone in this class could rebuild your website in a weekend with the same AI tool. So the code is **not** your competitive advantage.

- What about your business is **valuable, rare, hard to imitate and well-organized** (VRIN)?
- What would a competitor with a bigger budget need to copy you, and how long would it take?
- Which of your advantages get *stronger* over time (data, network, brand, switching costs)?

A competitor with a larger budget could potentially copy the application and advertise it to customers. However, they would still need to recruit technicians, establish trust, collect customer reviews and build a reliable network.
The competitive advantages that would become stronger over time are network effects, data and brand recognition. As more technicians and customers join, the platform becomes more useful. Over time, accumulated customer reviews and repair histories could also make it harder for competitors to offer the same level of service


## 5. Cloud and operations

What would it take to run this for real? Which parts would you buy as cloud services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), and what are the risks of relying on them?

I would use PaaS (Platform as a Service) to host and manage the application, customer accounts, technician listings and database. This would allow the platform to grow as more customers and technicians join without having to manage all the underlying infrastructure.

There are also risks associated with relying on cloud services. These include service outages, cybersecurity threats, customer information being compromised and increasing subscription costs as the platform grows. The business would also depend on third-party providers to keep important parts of the application operating.

To reduce these risks, I would use secure payment processing, data backups, strong account security and reputable cloud providers. This would help protect customer information and keep the platform operating reliably.
